# Supplier Criticality
Supplier risk assessment should consider business criticality in addition to technical security posture. A cybersecurity weakness at a highly critical supplier may create greater enterprise impact than the same weakness at a readily replaceable low-criticality supplier.

# Sole-Source and Alternate Sourcing
Sole-source suppliers create concentrated dependency because an equivalent supplier cannot be activated immediately. Critical sole-source relationships should have documented contingency, substitution, inventory, or recovery strategies. Alternate sourcing should be qualified before a disruption where feasible.

# Lead Time and Inventory Buffer
Long replenishment lead times and low inventory buffers reduce the time available to recover from supplier disruption. Resilience planning should consider lead time, on-hand inventory, safety stock, substitution, and recovery priorities together.

# Logistics Concentration
Reliance on a single port, carrier, freight lane, geography, or transport mode can increase common-mode disruption risk. Critical supply flows should identify practical alternate lanes or modes where possible.
