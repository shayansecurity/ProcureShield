# NIST C-SCRM Alignment
NIST SP 800-161 Rev. 1 describes cybersecurity supply chain risk management as an organizational risk-management activity spanning supplier identification, assessment, mitigation, monitoring, and response. ProcureShield uses this guidance as a conceptual reference rather than claiming that its prototype score is a NIST-prescribed formula.

# CSF 2.0 Governance
NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 includes the GOVERN function and emphasizes cybersecurity risk governance, roles, policy, oversight, and supply-chain risk management. Supplier assessments should therefore support documented decisions, ownership, escalation, and repeatable review rather than only technical scanning.

# Due Diligence and Monitoring
Supplier due diligence should examine the context of the relationship, access, data, service criticality, evidence, and downstream dependencies. Higher-risk suppliers warrant stronger assessment, contractual requirements, monitoring, and reassessment.

# Fourth-Party Dependencies
A supplier may depend on subcontractors, cloud providers, logistics providers, or other service organizations. Material downstream dependencies can create inherited risk and concentration risk even when the direct supplier appears acceptable.
